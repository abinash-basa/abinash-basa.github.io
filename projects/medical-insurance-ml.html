Statistical & ML Approaches for Medical Insurance Cost Prediction

An empirical investigation comparing classical inferential statistics with modern tree-based ensemble algorithms to analyze healthcare cost drivers and optimize predictive accuracy.

Domain Healthcare Analytics & ML
Top Model Performance Gradient Boosting (R² = 0.878)
Techniques ANOVA, OLS, RF, GBM, SVR
Dataset 1,338 Records, 7 Variables
Contents Research Problem Statistical Methodology Model Comparison Key Findings Academic Paper PDF

Research Problem & Objectives

Individual healthcare expenditures exhibit severe variance driven by non-linear interactions among demographic, behavioral, and clinical attributes. While classical statistical models offer transparent hypothesis testing, they often underfit complex interaction effects.

The objective of this dissertation was twofold:

  • Determine which demographic and lifestyle variables have statistically significant effects on individual medical charges.
  • Empirically benchmark five distinct regression and machine learning architectures to evaluate the tradeoff between interpretability and predictive precision.

Statistical Testing & Variable Architecture

The study utilized the Medical Cost Personal Dataset (1,338 records, 7 variables: age, sex, bmi, children, smoker, region, and charges).

Hypothesis Testing Highlights

Two-sample t-tests and one-way ANOVA confirmed that smoking status (p < 0.001) and BMI categories represent the most statistically dominant drivers of high charges, while biological sex and geographical region exhibited marginal direct variance.

Model Benchmark & Results

  • Gradient Boosting Regressor: Top overall performance (R² = 0.878), effectively capturing non-linear interactions between BMI and smoking status.
  • Random Forest Regressor: Strong second-best performer (R² ~ 0.861), exhibiting robust generalization across cross-validation folds.
  • Decision Tree Regressor: Exhibited higher variance and susceptibility to local overfitting (R² ~ 0.730).
  • Multiple Linear Regression (OLS): Provided transparent baseline coefficients (R² ~ 0.750) but missed high-cost compounding tails.
  • Support Vector Regression (SVR): Sensitive to kernel scaling on raw non-linear price curves.

Product & Actuarial Implications

From a product management and health-tech underwriting perspective, pure linear scoring tables materially under-price risk for high-BMI smokers. Incorporating non-linear boosting ensembles into underwriting tools enables actuarial teams to tailor risk-adjusted policy pricing while identifying high-risk cohorts for preventive wellness interventions.
