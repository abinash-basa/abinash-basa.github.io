Mathematical Model for Sleep Quality & Recovery Dynamics

Formulating first-order differential equations and applying Euler's numerical simulations to model non-linear cognitive recovery during continuous versus fragmented sleep.

Course M.Sc. Mathematics Term Paper
Focus Area Ordinary Differential Equations
Method Analytical & Euler's Simulation
Institution Lovely Professional University
Abstract & Context Mathematical Formulation Numerical Simulations Insights & Takeaways Paper PDF

Abstract and Problem Context

Sleep debt recovery is not a strictly linear process. The rate at which the brain recovers cognitive alertness depends on accumulated debt, sleep continuity, and circadian timing.

Written during my 3rd semester of M.Sc. Mathematics at Lovely Professional University, this term paper develops a mathematical model to examine how cognitive recovery changes over time during different sleep patterns.

Mathematical Formulation and ODE Model

The recovery rate was formulated using first-order ordinary differential equations representing the rate of change of cognitive deficit D(t) over continuous recovery time t:

Differential Equation Model

dD/dt = -λ D(t) + S(t)
Where λ is the recovery rate coefficient and S(t) accounts for circadian effects. The analytical solution exhibits exponential decay of cognitive debt during continuous rest.

Numerical Simulation and Euler's Method

To compare continuous sleep with fragmented sleep schedules, the differential equations were solved numerically using Euler's method across multiple parameter variations.

The simulations show how fragmented sleep results in less net recovery than an uninterrupted block of sleep of the same total duration, due to restart friction in each cycle.

Key Insights and Applications

  • Non-Linear Clearance: The highest rate of cognitive recovery occurs during the earliest continuous hours of rest.
  • Applied Mathematical Modeling: Illustrates how differential equations provide concrete insights into biological and cognitive systems.
